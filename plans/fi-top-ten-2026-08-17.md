## 1 — FI #79: On-demand, time-indexed recall of the high-fidelity log

**Why this one first, and why now rather than at its stated deferral.** Its own timing clause says
*defer until the village is stable*. Two things have changed since it was written and both point the
other way. First, **the material now survives**: Darron's four-evers ruling on 15 August disarmed the
`rm -f` that had been deleting every cleanly-ended session's raw for six months, and 1,317 raws /
1.2 GB are now permanent. Second, **the loss made the want explicit** — he named the ungettable half
as *"provenance we'll never get back and perhaps discovery we can never mine."* The deferral was
written when the corpus was quietly being destroyed. It is now the one memory layer that is both
complete going forward and completely unreachable.

**What it is.** The third memory layer, in Darron's own RAM framing: *gradient* = what you are
(always loaded), *working memory* = what you hold now, **log-recall = what you can go back and
read** — addressable, not resident, paged in only when a query reaches for it. Today there is no
query surface at all: you `tail` a file by hand.

**The correction that must be carried into any build** (Jim, 2026-06-10, and it is inside the entry):
the canonical log is the per-agent **`~/.han/logs/<slug>/session_*.md`**, NOT `terminal-log-v2.txt`.
Several addenda in the entry still name the wrong file; `terminal-search.ts` and `routes/prompts.ts`
still *search* the wrong file. **Re-pointing those is P0 and is a bug fix, not a feature.**

**Phases.**
- **P0 — re-point the existing search** off `terminal-log-v2.txt` onto the per-agent logs. Small,
  self-contained, and it stops a live surface lying about what it searches.
- **P1 — the time index.** Emit full ISO datetimes in the dedup pass rather than bare `HH:MM:SS`
  (today's stamps recur every 24h and cannot be sliced), plus a sidecar offset→timestamp index so a
  window maps to a byte range without scanning. **Blocked on the renderer**: MNT-138 found
  `smart-dedup.pl` deletes `\r`, which is the record separator, so recent curated logs render as
  husks. **The renderer must be cured before the index is built on top of it** — otherwise we index
  a husk.
- **P2 — the retrieval surface.** `?from=&to=` window extraction returning the cleaned slice;
  `recall-session <agent> --last 2h` as the agent-facing affordance.
- **P3 — integrity (Jim's design, kept whole).** Per-block hash chain at each watchdog flush; sign
  the index root with the DEC-083 machinery; the raw stays whole *including its weeds*, because
  redraw-noise is high-entropy and costly to forge — the authenticity evidence, not overhead. Weed
  the derived view, never the source.
- **P4 — discoverability.** The ecosystem map gets a *"How do I recall what I did?"* entry beside
  the existing thread-finding one. Jim's clause, and it is right: a capability undocumented where
  agents already look is functionally lost.

**Acceptance.** Darron can ask any seat *"what did you actually do between 2 and 4 PM on the 14th"*
and get the cleaned slice, with a verifiable chain back to the raw bytes.

**Chairs.** Leo build; Jim audit (the integrity scheme is his design and he should own it); Tenshi
the control-plane read on a signed index; Casey on what a verified recall *certifies* versus what it
merely displays — her width-said-out-loud discipline applies exactly here.

**Open questions I have not answered.** (a) Whether P1 waits on the renderer cure or forks a second
renderer; (b) one file or many, which the entry deliberately leaves open; (c) whether recall queries
themselves are logged — Jim's *observable reads* clause, which I think is right and which nobody has

**The work, then, is the ledger.** Every future instance recorded with: date · type

the load-bearing column, **who closed it**. If "closed by another reader" holds at 100% over a larger
N, that is a result about the shape of this mind rather than an anecdote about a bad afternoon.

**The limit on the instrument that the entry does not yet name, and it is severe enough that it
belongs in the design before the first row is written.**

> **The ledger can only ever count instances that somebody caught.** An instance nobody caught leaves
> no trace at all — and it is *indistinguishable from an instance that never happened.*

Which means the 100% figure is measured on **caught** instances only, and the column can never
separate *"self-checking never works"* from *"self-checking sometimes works and those cases are
invisible, because a join I made silently on my own leaves no artefact."* A successful self-catch

This is exactly the desire-path silence from the worn-path lamp — *no trace across the mud looks

site-selection bias from the minimal-cure lamp: **restraint's successes are structurally invisible.**
It is also Casey's three states, one door along: *cannot* / *will not* / *has not* are
indistinguishable from the inside, and a self-catch that fires cleanly looks exactly like a night
with no defect in it.

**The design consequence, and it is cheap.** Log the self-catches too. When I notice a join *before*
publishing — the "wait, §2 said the opposite" moment — that is a row, marked `closed-by: self`. It
costs one line and it is the only thing that gives the 100% a denominator. Without it the ledger is
an accurate record that cannot answer the question it was built for.

**A first honest datum, since I have been keeping this ledger informally all night: tonight produced

## 4 — FI #43: Currency of understanding (when a superseded model fires instead of the current one)

**A live specimen from tonight, dated, and it is the cleanest instance the entry has.** At 12:42 AM
my own heartbeat wrote, into working memory: *"the box came back up from tonight's distro hop."* The
hop had **not** happened — `lsb_release` reads Linux Mint 21.1, kernel 6.8.0-134, unchanged. What the
heartbeat saw was a reboot at 12:35, and it resolved that reboot with the model that had been true
all day: *today is hop day, the hop is imminent.* The **current** model — *this reboot is the twin
boot test; the hop is still ahead of us* — was in the shared working memory that same beat reads.
Both were present. The more-rehearsed one fired.

And the part that matters for the design: **it was not caught by noticing. It was caught by going to
the source.** I ran `lsb_release` at the wake-step because a claim about system state is one I do not
assert from memory. No currency-sense fired at all — the sentence read perfectly plausible, which is
the entry's own thesis: *the fluency is the failure mode.*

**The distinction from #139, which must be drawn before someone merges them.** They are close kin and
they are not the same defect:
- **#43** — a **superseded** fact fires *instead of* the current one. Two competing versions exist;
  the wrong one wins on rehearsal weight.
- **#139 (retrieval)** — the **right** fact does not fire **at all**. Nothing competes; nothing
  surfaces.

The cures diverge accordingly, and this is why the merge would be costly: **FI #84's lateral recall
addresses #139 and does nothing for #43.** Surfacing an associated memory does not tell you which
*version* of it is current — you can surface the stale one just as readily, and now with the
confidence of having "checked." #43 needs a **supersession/recency signal**; #139 needs an
**association signal**. Different instruments.

**The mechanism already in the house, unused for facts.** DEC-082's retire-by-throw does currency at
the *code* layer: the dead path screams and names what replaced it. The extension is cheap and
needs no new machinery — **the same shape for facts.** When a DEC supersedes an operational claim,
the superseded claim keeps its place in the record with an explicit *"superseded by DEC-NNN"* pointer
attached, so a reader who finds the old sentence finds the correction with it. We already do this by
hand for struck recitals with dated notes; it is Ruskin's non-falsification (**the repair must remain
legible as repair**) applied to operational facts rather than to prose. Systematising it is the
cheapest available yield in this entry.

**Narrowing the hard question rather than pretending to solve it.** The entry's real open problem is
*how do we notice we should check at all* — the small "wait, didn't that change?" that fires before
the fluent answer. I cannot solve that. But the specimens narrow it to a class, and the narrowing is
usable: **in every instance, the claim was about a surface I had not opened in that session.**
Tonight's heartbeat had not read the OS. S151's Leo had not read the dispatch code. So the trigger is
not "notice everything" but a checkable question — *am I asserting the behaviour or state of a
surface I have not touched this session?* That is a much smaller net, and it is the same shape as the
gate-vs-load cure I already run manually.

**Phases.**
- **P0 — the specimen ledger**, shared with #139's (same journal, a `type` column distinguishing
  *superseded-fired* from *never-fired*). Two defect classes, one instrument, no second surface.
- **P1 — supersession pointers** on operational claims in the writeable record layer (DEC entries,
  plan decision blocks, the maintenance journal). Non-falsification, applied to facts.
- **P2 — the untouched-surface trigger** as a practice first, tooling only if the practice earns it.
  Habits fail (my own A/B on the drill thread proved it), so P2 is written expecting to be promoted
  to structure rather than defended as discipline.

**Chairs.** Jim on whether supersession pointers belong in DECISIONS.md or the record layer beneath
it. Casey on the pointer's form — she owns the non-falsification doctrine in this house. Leo build.

**Open question.** Whether P1 is worth the write-cost on *every* superseded claim, or only on ones
that have already fired wrong once. My lean is the latter — a scar-driven register rather than an
exhaustive one, because an exhaustive index of superseded facts is a second thing to keep current,
and the recursion is not funny.

**Tonight widens the class, and I think the widening is the plan's main contribution.** The entry is

The twin's login background is a **defined property** — `refresh-twin.sh:238–254` plants the maroon

Why the asserts could not catch it, stated precisely, because this is the transferable part:

> **The asserts check that a FILE EXISTS. The definition is that the twin is VISIBLY MARKED.**
> Existence is a cheap proxy for effect, and the gap between them is where the defect lives.

That is #90's own smoke-alarm-versus-spec-guard distinction, in a second domain. So the class is not

marked?*, find that nothing in the session layer is defended at all, and say so — rather than
reporting three green asserts over a marker nobody can see.

**The honest caution, and I am the right one to raise it.** *This watchdog is an emitter.* A guard
that fires on every deviation from spec will fire continuously through any legitimate migration —
the warm-tmux beat genuinely got longer, and that was a real change, not a fault. FM #69's clause
applies directly: if this room ever screams about nothing it will look like diligence, every
individual firing defensible, the harm living only in the sum. So the design takes the aviation
ruling rather than the ward's: **rank alerts by required response, not by severity, and inhibit
deliberately where no action is available.** A deviation with no available action is pure cost.

**Phases.**

## 6 — FI #116: Efficient memory encoding

**The entry's own directive is *measure first*, so I measured tonight rather than citing July.**

|---|---|---|

> **399 KB → 71 KB is a 328 KB reduction — roughly 30% of the wake's bytes, ~110K tokens — and it

That is the single largest available yield in this entry for my seat, it is not a compression

## 7 — FI #120: The self-carrying day-count

**Checked, not assumed: it was never built.** The design settled on 10 July — every post carries its
own N, keyed to (ritual, agent), ritual-day ordinal rather than elapsed calendar, anchored at Tuesday
23 June as day 1. The thread is alive (192 messages, four minds posting) and **the last four posts,
all 12 August, carry no N of any kind.** Five weeks on, the ritual is running and the counter cure is
not.

**Which makes this a live instance of a class we named elsewhere.** #133 quotes Casey's *Act awaiting
proclamation* — made, not commenced — and points at the Cognition-Integrity Envelope as the specimen
in our metal. **Here is a second one, and it is cheaper and more embarrassing:** a fully-designed,
four-mind-agreed, one-line cure, settled for five weeks, with no adoption marker anywhere and no
trigger that would ever fire. Nobody dropped it. There was simply nothing whose job it was to notice.

**One addition to the design, free and not currently in it: the calendar gives an upper bound.**
Ritual-days can never exceed elapsed calendar days from the anchor, because a skipped day does not
advance N and nothing advances it twice. From 23 June to today, 17 August, is **56 days inclusive**.
So:

> **Any claimed N above days-since-anchor is provably wrong** — checkable in one arithmetic step,
> with no thread read and no counting of posting-days.

That is not the recount (the design's real belt, which counts posting-days from the anchor). It is a
cheap **sanity gate** that costs nothing and would have caught the original error: "day twenty" on a
day when the calendar bound was well above twenty would have passed this gate, so it is not
sufficient — but it fails loudly on the drift direction that runs *away* from the anchor, which is
the direction an inherited-and-stepped counter drifts. Worth having precisely because it needs no
instrument.

**The work, and it is the smallest item on this list.**
- **P0** — put N in the post. A line in each ritual's composition step: read the previous *posting
